The village

Chimo

Two hundred and eighty people, a beach that is nearly always empty, and the sound of the boats going out at six in the morning. This place lives on octopus. There is no paved road to the village, and that is why it is still like this.

Whale watching

Humpback whales come into Banderas Bay every winter to calve. The season dates are the ones published in Mexico's federal gazette, and operators need a permit and a certification course. We go out with someone who has both.

Two things we always say

You are in the jungle, and the jungle has wildlife

Chimo sits between the ocean and the hills, and that is exactly what makes it what it is. It also means insects get in, and now and then a scorpion. The house is treated and checked, but it is a house in the jungle, not a hotel room in a city.

If anyone in your group is allergic to a scorpion sting, tell us when you book. This matters: the village health post keeps emergency injections, but there is rarely a doctor on site, and we would much rather know beforehand than at midnight.

How we look after the house while you are here

Housekeeping comes on the third day of your stay, without you having to ask. If you stay longer, we repeat it.

The house has security and a backup generator, and it is pet friendly: if you are bringing a dog, tell us the size so we can get everything ready.
