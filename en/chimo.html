Chimo

A fishing village in Cabo Corrientes, at the southern end of Banderas Bay.

What this place is

Two hundred and eighty people and thirty kilometres of coast

Chimo is a locality in the municipality of Cabo Corrientes, Jalisco. About 280 people live here year-round, though the number rises during fishing season and if you count the nearby ranches. It sits roughly 30 kilometres south of Puerto Vallarta along the coastline.

You hear the ocean all day. Fishing is the main activity and what gets sold is octopus and fish. The village pier has been half-built for more than twenty years, which limits how much can be landed. There is a health post, though there is rarely a doctor there.

The beaches stay empty most of the year because getting here takes effort. That is exactly the point.

What there is and what there isn't

We would rather tell you now than have you find out on arrival.

There is

  • Beach kitchens in the village
  • Fresh fish and octopus
  • A local health post
  • Water and power at the house
  • Beaches that stay empty

There isn't

  • ATM
  • Pharmacy
  • Supermarket
  • Full-time doctor
  • Paved road to the door

Nearby

What is close

Yelapa

The most visited village on the south shore. Same water, far more people.

Quimixto

A waterfall and horseback rides. Written up as the most untouched village in Mexico.

Majahuitas

A small beach reachable only by sea.

Las Ánimas

First stop on the corridor, with beach restaurants.

Playa Corrales

Heavier surf, and a lighthouse built in 1902.

Tehuamixtle

A bay between cliffs, known for its oysters. A shrimp boat that sank in 1920 is dived here.

Mayto

An open 12 km beach with a sea turtle camp running since 2005.

El Tuito

The municipal seat at 1,085 m, five to ten degrees cooler. Panela cheese, coffee, honey and raicilla.

Before you come

What to bring

  • Cash in pesos. There is no ATM.
  • Any medication you need. There is no pharmacy.
  • Repellent and sunscreen.
  • Closed shoes if you plan to walk to the waterfalls.
  • A power bank.
  • A dry bag if you are taking gear out on the boat.
